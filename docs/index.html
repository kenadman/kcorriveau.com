When Leaders Call Me

When should a company bring in a fractional or interim CIO?

Bring in a fractional or interim CIO when technology leadership has a gap the business cannot afford to wait on. The usual triggers are a departed technology leader, a PE sponsor asking for a technology and AI plan, an acquisition to integrate, or a stack of AI pilots with no P&L to show for them. A fractional CIO gives you senior leadership part-time, and an interim CIO runs the function full-time for a defined period.

  • Your technology leader has left, or you have lost confidence in the plan.
  • Your PE sponsor wants a technology and AI story before the next board meeting.
  • An acquisition needs to be integrated without breaking the business.
  • Everyone is piloting AI, and nobody can say which pilot moves EBITDA.

My View on AI

Who should own AI inside a company?

In my view, the CIO. AI touches data, security, vendors and every workflow the CIO already owns, so splitting it into a separate seat usually adds a handoff, not an outcome. Most AI initiatives stall on people and integration rather than technology: MIT’s NANDA initiative found that about 95% of enterprise generative AI pilots (opens in a new tab) delivered little or no measurable P&L impact.

Source: MIT NANDA, State of AI in Business 2025, as reported by Fortune.

What is the difference between a fractional, interim and full-time CIO?

A fractional CIO provides senior technology leadership part-time, an interim CIO runs the function full-time for a defined period, and a full-time CIO is a permanent hire. The right choice depends on how urgent the gap is and how much leadership time the business needs.

Fractional, interim and full-time CIO compared
Fractional CIO Interim CIO Full-time CIO
Time commitment Part-time, often one to two days a week Full-time for a defined period Full-time and permanent
Best for Senior leadership without a full-time executive A leadership gap, transition or turnaround A company ready for a permanent technology executive
Typical focus Strategy, governance, roadmap, vendors and team guidance Running the function day to day while setting direction Long-term ownership of technology and AI

Why do so many AI initiatives fail to deliver?

MIT’s NANDA initiative found that about 95% of enterprise generative AI pilots (opens in a new tab) delivered little or no measurable P&L impact, and it pointed to integration and learning gaps rather than model quality. In my experience, the failure sits in the gap between what the C-suite believes and what employees actually do. The fix is to measure both levels, prioritize the few moves that matter, and sequence them with owners.

Does a company need a Chief AI Officer?

IBM’s Institute for Business Value (opens in a new tab) reports that 26% of organizations now have a Chief AI Officer, and that those organizations report about 10% higher ROI from AI. My read is that the gain comes from having one accountable owner for AI. For most mid-market companies, that owner should be an upgraded CIO rather than a new seat in the C-suite, because the Chief AI Officer is this cycle’s Chief Digital Officer.
